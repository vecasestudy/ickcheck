Camera & privacy

Ick Check has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. Here is exactly what it does with your camera and your data.

Your camera only turns on when you tap Start camera, and it turns off after a scan, when you tap Stop camera, or when you leave the Scan tab.

Photos never leave your phone. Barcode photos and label photos are read on your phone and are not uploaded or stored.

What leaves your phone

  • Barcode numbers and search words are sent to Open Beauty Facts, an open product database, to look up the product. Their privacy policy applies to those requests.
  • Nothing else. No photos, no name, no email, no location.

What stays on your phone

  • Your scorecard and labels you saved are kept in your browser’s storage on this device only. Clearing your browser data removes them.
  • Share links contain your scorecard (product names and flags) inside the link itself. Anyone you send a link to can see that scorecard.

Turn camera access off any time

  1. iPhone, just this site: in Safari, tap aA in the address bar, then Website Settings, then Camera, and choose Ask or Deny.
  2. iPhone, all sites: Settings, then Apps, then Safari, then Camera.
  3. Android (Chrome): tap the icon to the left of the web address, then Permissions, then Camera.

Opened Ick Check inside LinkedIn, Instagram or another app? Those built-in browsers can block the camera. Open the page in Safari or Chrome instead.

What the flags mean

Flags come from the EU endocrine disruptor lists (edlists.org). They are not safety verdicts or medical advice. Product data comes from a community database and can be incomplete, so check results against your label.
