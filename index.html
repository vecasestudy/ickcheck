What it does

Ick Check shows you what’s really in your beauty and personal care products, right from your phone.

  • 01

    Scan or paste a label

    Point your camera at the barcode, or paste the ingredient list from the back of the bottle.

  • 02

    See what got flagged, and who flagged it

    Each flagged ingredient names the public list it comes from. No mystery scores.

  • 03

    Share your routine scorecard

    Add products to one scorecard and send it to the group chat or pin it.

Flags are not safety verdicts or medical advice.

Background

After I participated in a former accelerator, doing research on Gen Z, I’ve been obsessed with sustainability and longevity. There is an incredible amount of misinformation and mislabeling in many of our consumer products and goods, and the beauty industry is no different. For personal reasons, I began to dive deep into the products I was using, particularly endocrine disruptors, because coming out of COVID left me with an incredible amount of energy depletion, emotionally, physically, and mentally. I needed to do everything possible to get back to my energetic self. I might be a Millennial, but out of the research, Gen Z cares just as much about their self-care as we do, or at least the portion of Millennials who are not completely “agro.” In fact, 43% of Gen Z look for that “Non-Toxic” label and absolutely detest “greenwashing.” In essence, they are asking for “the receipt” from their products, with an increased use of ingredient-checker apps. And since they lean toward physical retail for the experience, it certainly helps to ensure everything checks out. You’re welcome.

And Gen Z, I know you didn’t ask me to do anything, but I needed it just as much as you, so I hope you get some benefit.

Adjacent competitors

  • Routine and skin-type tools

    Good Face, Skinsort, Skincarisma. These focus on what works for your skin, not just what’s flagged. Good Face also lets users share.

  • Pregnancy and baby safety scanners

    Oli and Little Bean came up in my search. They cover the fertility angle, so if I go that way, these are the ones to study. I haven’t reviewed them in detail.

  • Retailer tools

    Sephora’s app has an AI skin scan. Retailers could add ingredient flags at any point.

Where the category is weak, and where Ick Check fits

  • Scores are under fire.

    A Marie Claire investigation quotes cosmetic chemists criticizing these apps. They judge ingredients without knowing the dose, flag approved preservatives like phenoxyethanol as “poor,” and disagree with each other. The same CeraVe cleanser scored “poor” on Yuka but green on INCI Beauty. Ick Check’s choice to show flags with a named source and no score answers that criticism directly.

  • Almost none are social.

    They’re built for private checking in a store aisle. A shareable scorecard designed for Gen Z, Pinterest and group chats is Ick Check’s real growth angle.

  • No download needed.

    Ick Check opens instantly from a link. The others are installed apps.
